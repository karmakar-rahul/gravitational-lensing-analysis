# Gravitational Lensing Analysis of Black Holes and Wormholes

## Overview

This project presents a computational study of gravitational lensing phenomena in compact astrophysical objects, with a focus on comparing the optical signatures of Schwarzschild Black Holes, Kalb-Ramond Black Holes, and Morris-Thorne Wormholes.

The work is based on the principles of General Relativity and Modified Gravity theories, where the propagation of light in curved spacetime is investigated through analytical and numerical methods. Key observables such as photon sphere radius, shadow radius, and gravitational deflection angle are analyzed to identify potential signatures that may distinguish black holes from traversable wormholes.

The project was originally developed as part of the Master's dissertation in Physics (Astrophysics Specialization) and later converted into a computational research repository using Python.

---

## Research Objectives

The primary objectives of this project are:

* Study gravitational lensing around Schwarzschild Black Holes.
* Investigate the effects of Kalb-Ramond gravity on optical observables.
* Analyze Morris-Thorne Wormhole geometries.
* Compare lensing signatures between black holes and wormholes.
* Visualize the behavior of photon trajectories under different spacetime configurations.
* Explore observational indicators that may help distinguish exotic compact objects.

---

## Physical Systems Investigated

### Schwarzschild Black Hole

The Schwarzschild metric represents a static, spherically symmetric, non-rotating black hole solution to Einstein's Field Equations.

Parameters studied:

* Schwarzschild Radius
* Photon Sphere Radius
* Shadow Radius
* Weak Deflection Angle

### Kalb-Ramond Black Hole

The Kalb-Ramond field introduces corrections to General Relativity through an antisymmetric tensor field naturally emerging in String Theory.

Parameters studied:

* Kalb-Ramond Deformation Parameter (Γ)
* Coupling Parameter (λ)
* Modified Photon Sphere Radius
* Modified Shadow Radius
* Modified Weak Deflection Angle

### Morris-Thorne Wormhole

The Morris-Thorne Wormhole provides a traversable wormhole solution connecting two asymptotically flat regions of spacetime.

Parameters studied:

* Shape Function
* Wormhole Throat Geometry
* Effective Photon Potential
* Deflection Angle
* Lensing Signatures

## Key Findings

* Increasing Kalb-Ramond deformation parameters modify the optical structure of black holes.
* Photon sphere radius decreases with increasing deformation strength.
* Shadow radius exhibits measurable deviations from the Schwarzschild case.
* Wormholes can produce qualitatively different lensing signatures due to the absence of an event horizon.
* Comparative lensing analysis demonstrates the potential of gravitational lensing as a tool for identifying exotic compact objects.
