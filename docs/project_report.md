# Detailed Project Report

## Abstract

Gravitational lensing is one of the most powerful observational tools available for studying compact astrophysical objects and testing gravitational theories. The present work investigates the optical properties of Schwarzschild Black Holes, Kalb-Ramond Black Holes, and Morris-Thorne Wormholes through a comparative lensing analysis.

The study focuses on three major observables:

1. Photon Sphere Radius
2. Shadow Radius
3. Weak Deflection Angle

Computational models were developed using Python to evaluate the influence of modified gravity parameters on these observables and to compare lensing signatures between black holes and wormholes.

---

# Introduction

The curvature of spacetime predicted by General Relativity causes light rays to deviate from straight-line trajectories. This phenomenon, known as gravitational lensing, serves as a direct probe of spacetime geometry around massive objects.

Black holes and wormholes represent two fundamentally different classes of compact objects. While black holes possess event horizons, traversable wormholes provide hypothetical connections between distant regions of spacetime.

Understanding their optical signatures is therefore essential for future observational identification.

---

# Theoretical Framework

## Schwarzschild Geometry

The Schwarzschild solution describes the external spacetime surrounding a static and spherically symmetric mass.

Major observables investigated:

* Schwarzschild Radius
* Photon Sphere
* Shadow Radius
* Deflection Angle

---

## Kalb-Ramond Gravity

Kalb-Ramond gravity introduces additional fields beyond Einstein's General Relativity and naturally arises within low-energy limits of String Theory.

The modified metric introduces two parameters:

* Gamma (Γ)
* Lambda (λ)

These parameters modify the curvature structure and consequently alter photon trajectories.

---

## Morris-Thorne Wormhole

The Morris-Thorne metric provides a traversable wormhole solution characterized by:

* Redshift Function
* Shape Function
* Throat Radius

Unlike black holes, wormholes lack an event horizon, leading to distinct lensing characteristics.

# Results

The simulations indicate that:

* Photon sphere radius decreases with increasing deformation parameters.
* Shadow radius shows measurable departures from the Schwarzschild solution.
* Deflection angles vary systematically with modified gravity parameters.
* Wormhole geometries generate lensing behavior distinct from conventional black holes.

Embedding diagrams further illustrate the geometric structure of traversable wormholes.

---

# Conclusion

This study demonstrates the usefulness of gravitational lensing as a diagnostic tool for investigating both conventional and exotic compact objects.

The computational framework provides a foundation for future studies involving:

* Strong lensing
* Ray tracing
* Rotating spacetimes
* Numerical geodesic integration
* Modified gravity theories

The project highlights how observational signatures can be employed to distinguish black holes from wormholes and to test extensions of General Relativity.
